The claim

c is fully lit exactly when g is prime — it spans one whole period of gcd(·,g) minus the single point r=g, so it holds precisely φ(g) coprime points.

d is fully lit whenever s is prime, but it samples only g−1 residues of the longer period s, so below the threshold it can come up full on a composite s.

For g > √E the leak closes: a composite s has a prime factor ≤ √s, which a window of g−1 consecutive integers cannot miss. There, both strips full ⟺ g and s both prime.

Verified exhaustively for every G < 300: zero counterexamples above the threshold, and every false positive below it.
